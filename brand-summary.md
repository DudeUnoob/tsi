Brand Overview
2
Introduction
This document introduces the foundational 
elements of the Sanga identity system, including 
visual direction, communication principles, and 
a series of example applications across social 
media, events, merchandise, and digital spaces.
The aim of this project was not to create a 
rigid or overly technical brand system, but 
to establish a clear and usable foundation 
that reflects how Sanga already operates 
and is experienced by its members.
The examples shown throughout this document 
should therefore be treated as practical 
reference points. Together, they demonstrate 
the intended tone, pacing, typography, colour 
relationships, and overall visual character of 
the identity in realistic day-to-day scenarios.
At this stage, consistency is more valuable than 
constant reinvention. Rather than creating entirely 
new visual approaches for every event or 
communication, the strongest results will usually 
come from working closely within the patterns, 
structures, and restraint already established here.
The application examples included are 
intentionally selective rather than exhaustive. 
They focus on the primary ways Sanga 
currently presents itself – retreats, gatherings, 
social communication, merchandise, and 
ongoing connection across the year.
As the identity begins to be used more 
actively, new situations and requirements 
will naturally emerge. The intention is not to 
prevent experimentation, but to encourage 
thoughtful development built from a 
strong and consistent starting point.

Understanding Sanga
4
What Sanga Is
Sanga is a Vaishnava Y outh Collective for 
friendship, growth, and shared experience.
Sanga brings together young people exploring 
Krishna consciousness through friendship, 
conversation, and shared experience.
Through retreats, gatherings, and ongoing 
connection, members build relationships 
that carry into everyday life.
The experience is shaped as much by the people 
as the events themselves – through discussion, 
travel, reflection, humour, music, service, and time 
spent together across different stages of life.
Sanga is not built around performance, 
image, or recruitment. It exists to create 
meaningful association between people 
navigating spiritual life together.
Shared experience
Retreats, gatherings, discussions, travel, and 
everyday moments create a sense of connection 
that continues beyond individual events.
Personal growth
Members are encouraged to ask questions, 
explore ideas openly, and move forward 
in spiritual life at their own pace.
Lasting connection
What begins at Sanga often continues 
through friendships, smaller circles, 
mentorship, collaboration, and ongoing 
support across different stages of life.
Understanding Sanga
5
Brand Principles
People before polish
Sanga should always feel human, welcoming, 
and grounded in real relationships.
Moments of connection matter more than 
perfectly curated presentation. Communications 
should reflect genuine interaction, shared 
experience, and participation rather than trying 
to appear overly produced or promotional.
Contemporary , not trend-driven
The identity should feel current and recognisable 
without relying on short-lived visual trends.
Typography, colour, imagery, and layouts 
should aim for longevity rather than novelty, 
allowing the brand to grow naturally over 
time without needing constant reinvention.
Consistent, not repetitive
Recognition comes through repeated 
structure, tone, and restraint – not through 
making every post look identical.
Consistency should come from shared principles, 
typography, spacing, and hierarchy rather than 
rigid templates or over-controlled systems.
Built around participation
Sanga exists to bring people together.
Communications should encourage involvement, 
conversation, travel, reflection, friendship, and 
shared experience rather than functioning 
purely as announcements or broadcasts.
Warmth through restraint
The identity should feel expressive and 
relational without becoming overly 
sentimental or visually overloaded.
Simple typography, strong photography, 
thoughtful pacing, and clear messaging 
create a stronger sense of atmosphere than 
excessive decoration or constant visual noise.
Designed to be lived with
The identity should work naturally 
across everyday life.
Whether appearing on clothing, retreat 
materials, social media, presentations, or 
event communications, Sanga should feel 
comfortable, recognisable, and easy to carry 
into different environments and stages of life.
Understanding Sanga
6
Tone and Communication
Sanga communicates in a way that 
feels open, grounded, and human.
The tone should reflect the experience 
people already associate with the group: 
friendship, encouragement, shared experience, 
curiosity, and ongoing connection.
Communications do not need to sound 
overly polished, institutional, or heavily 
promotional. The aim is not to impress people 
with language, but to make people feel 
welcomed into something genuine and lived.
The tone should feel
• conversational rather than corporate
• optimistic without exaggeration
• thoughtful without becoming overly serious
• expressive without becoming sentimental
• contemporary without relying on trends or slang
• spiritually grounded without 
overusing devotional language
Writing principles
Keep communication:
• clear
• direct
• easy to understand
• visually balanced
• centred around people and experience
In most cases, shorter and simpler 
language will feel stronger and more 
natural than over-explaining.
A void
• excessive hype or marketing language
• overly formal or institutional phrasing
• trying to explain everything at once
• language that feels exclusive or performative
• over-decorating simple messages with 
too many graphics, fonts, or effects
Good communication tends to focus on
• the experience itself
• who people will meet
• what people will share together
• where and when something is happening
• how someone can take part
The strongest communications usually 
feel invitational, not promotional.
